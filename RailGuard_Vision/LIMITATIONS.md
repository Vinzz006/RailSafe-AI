# RailGuard Vision — Technical Limitations & Operational Boundaries

> **MANDATORY NOTICE:**  
> This software is an engineering and research prototype designed exclusively to provide spatial-temporal early-warning cues to human security personnel across Indian Railways premises.  
> **IT DOES NOT DETECT EXPLOSIVES, NARCOTICS, CHEMICAL RESIDUES, OR CONCEALED WEAPONS.**  
> Every alert generated by this system is strictly **FOR HUMAN REVIEW ONLY**.

---

## 1. Physical & Optical Sensor Limits

1. **Optical Opacity:**  
   Standard visible-spectrum cameras (webcams, CCTV, RTSP optical sensors) observe only reflected ambient light from external surfaces. Visible light cannot penetrate synthetic fabrics, leather, polycarbonate suitcases, or metal trunks.
2. **No Spectrometric or Chemical Detection:**  
   The system possesses zero chemical, radiological, or molecular detection capability. It cannot distinguish between a backpack containing ordinary clothing and one containing dangerous chemicals.
3. **Role as an Operator-Assistance Layer:**  
   RailGuard Vision serves solely as an initial triage filter. Its only function is to notify Railway Protection Force (RPF) / security operators of stationary unattended bags so personnel can deploy certified physical inspection methods:
   - Certified Multi-Energy Dual-View X-Ray Baggage Inspection Systems (XBIS).
   - Explosive Trace Detectors (ETD).
   - Trained Police Sniffer Canine Squads.

---

## 2. Computer Vision & Tracking Limitations

1. **Occlusion & Clutter:**  
   In dense railway station concourses or platform boarding stampedes, passengers walking between the camera and a bag cause visual occlusion. While temporal grace periods mitigate brief dropouts, prolonged occlusion may cause track loss.
2. **Track ID Switches:**  
   ByteTrack relies on bounding box overlap (IoU) and Kalman filter trajectory prediction. When two passengers or bags cross in close proximity, identity switches can occur.
3. **Perspective & Pixel Distortion:**  
   Distances are computed in 2D image coordinates (pixels). Without site-specific camera calibration and camera ground-plane homography, 140 pixels in the foreground represents a much shorter physical distance (e.g. ~1.2 meters) than 140 pixels in the distant background (e.g. ~6.0 meters).
4. **Camera Rigidity Requirement:**  
   The stationarity rules engine assumes a fixed, static camera mount. If an operator uses a handheld camera, pan-tilt-zoom (PTZ) tour, or vibrates the camera pole, static bags will register non-zero pixel displacements.
5. **Luggage Stacks & Shared Belongings:**  
   When multiple passengers travel together (e.g., families or tour groups) and place their luggage in a central cluster while sitting nearby, simple nearest-neighbor heuristics may associate the entire cluster with a single person.

---

## 3. Contextual Cues & Ethical Boundaries

1. **Loitering is Inherently Noisy:**  
   Indian Railways stations routinely host passengers with valid tickets awaiting delayed trains or resting between transfers for hours. Dwell time on platforms is heavily influenced by train schedules. **Loitering must never be interpreted as evidence of criminal or malicious intent.** In this system, loitering is tagged solely as a low-weight informational note, never an enforcement trigger.
2. **No Biometric or Facial Recognition:**  
   No facial recognition, emotion analysis, gait identification, or biometric classification is implemented. Track IDs (e.g., Track #12) are discarded when tracks exit the field of view.
3. **No Demographic Profiling:**  
   The detection model classifies standard object categories (`person`, `backpack`, `handbag`, `suitcase`). It does not infer age, gender, ethnicity, attire, or socio-economic background.

---

## 4. Operational False Alarm Rate Interpretation

The dashboard reports the historical **False Alarm Rate**:
$$\text{False Alarm Rate} = \frac{\text{False Alarm Reviews}}{\text{Total Reviewed Alerts}}$$

Operators must understand:
- This metric reflects the **historical proportion** of reviewed alerts deemed false alarms by operators.
- **It does NOT represent the probability that a specific incoming alert is wrong.**
- Every alert must be independently verified on its own merits before physical intervention.
